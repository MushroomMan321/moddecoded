Colossal Reactors Reactor Calculator

Uses the Colossal Reactors 26.1.2-2.4.1.0.0 reactor formulas

Paint rods and heat sinks, or lay out the same patterns the Reactor Builder uses, and see the RF/t or steam, fuel burn and turbine needs the controller will show. Pick FTB Evolution 2 under Modpack for that pack's config: it changes the numbers a lot.

Build guide

Build from the bottom up. Step through the layers with ◀ ▶ on the 3D view; it hides everything above the layer you're on.

How it goes together
  1. The outside is a closed box. Every outside block must be Reactor Casing, Reactor Glass, a port, or a Reactor Rod Controller. The guide puts casing on the edges and glass on the faces so you can see in; any mix works.
  2. Each Reactor Rod Controller sits in the roof, and every block under it down to the floor must be a Reactor Rod. A rod that isn't under a rod controller stops the reactor forming.
  3. The Reactor Controller goes outside the box, against any face. Exactly one: a second one stops it forming unless allowMultipleReactorControllers is on.
  4. Everything else inside must be air or a heat sink from the list. Any other block stops it forming. At most one Reactor Redstone Port.
  5. A Reactor Resource Port set to Insert takes fuel (and water); one set to Extract gives waste (and steam). The default filter handles both. Press Reboot on the controller after changing a port's mode or filter.
  6. With no coolant inside, the reactor sends RF out of a Reactor Power Port. Pipe water in and it turns its RF into steam instead.

From ReactorValidation.validate in the Colossal Reactors 26.1.2-2.4.1.0.0 jar.

Output

    Checked in game with the FTB Evolution 2 config: eight test reactors (air, gold, diamond, emerald, graphite, Ender Goo and Gelid Breezium, uranium and azurite, with and without water) matched the page's RF/t, steam and water to the unit, and fuel burn to four digits.

    Assumes fuel is kept topped up, waste is taken out, and the power port (or the steam port and its water supply) keeps up. Reactor instability is off by default and in FTB Evolution 2, so heat sinks' overheating value isn't used.

    What the code actually says

    Power only counts heat sinks touching a rod

    Every interior cell next to a rod is "adjacent". RF uses the average energy of those cells, and drops if there are fewer of them than rod blocks. Cells further out only change fuel burn, never RF.

    RF/t = baseRF × 0.8 × n^exp
       × avg energy (adjacent cells)
       × min(1, adjacent cells / rods) × 1.16
    ReactorSimulation.tick

    Each rod loses 25% per crowded side

    n above isn't your rod count. Each rod block loses a quarter for every side (of four) touching another rod or the casing. A checkerboard rod touches neither, so it counts in full; a rod against the wall in a corner counts half.

    n = Σ rods max(0, 1 − 0.25 × crowded sides)
    ReactorControllerBlockEntity.rebuildSimulationCaches

    More rods, less power each

    Power grows as n to a power below 1. The mod uses 0.9; FTB Evolution 2 sets 0.76, so doubling the effective rods there gives 1.69× the RF, not 2×. Fuel burn grows almost linearly, so small reactors get more RF per ingot.

    rodEnergyScalingExponent  0.9 → 0.76 (Evo 2)
    ReactorSimulation.rodEnergyScaling

    Water turns all of the power into steam

    With water piped in, the reactor boils RF × factor mB of water a tick and sends no RF at all while the water lasts. Only the share it can't boil comes out as RF.

    water/t = RF × 0.45   (Evo 2: 0.085)
steam/t = water × 1.0 (Evo 2: 3.93)
    ReactorSimulation.tick · reactor_coolant.json

    Fuel burn has its own curve

    Fuel use scales with n too, divided by a curve that flattens as the reactor grows, and by the weighted fuel value of every heat sink cell (cells not touching a rod count half).

    fuel/t = 0.03 × n × 1.72 / (n+1)^k
         / heatSinkFuel / 1.35 × 1.21
k = 0.5 × (0.4 + 0.5 × 400/(n+400))
    ReactorSimulation.tick

    Empty space stretches fuel more than it should

    Cells not touching a rod are meant to count for at most 4 per rod block. The controller caps their count but adds their fuel values in full, so a few rods in a big empty reactor burn far less fuel than the formula intends. In game, 9 rod columns in a 15×15×6 of air burned 0.178 units/t; the intended cap gives 0.353.

    capNonAdjacentHeatSinks(rods, countNon,
    new double[]{fuelNon}, …)  // copies, thrown away
    ReactorSimulation.computeHeatSinkModifiersFromStaticCache

    FTB Evolution 2's guide is out of date

    The pack's in-game guide still describes its 1.3.0 values. Pack 1.3.1 changed the config and the water coolant again; the calculator uses what the pack ships now (1.4.1).
