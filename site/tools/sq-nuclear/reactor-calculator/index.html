    What the code actually says

    The rods set how much water the core wants

    Each locked shaft asks for its share of 640 mB/t, scaled by how deep the rod is and how much charge is left in it, times its cladding tier. A full tier 1 core wants exactly 640. The moderator multiplies it, xenon and boron cut it.

    fuel  = Σ claddings remaining × tier ÷ 57,600
        tier 1 / 3.75 / 7.5 / 12.5, MOX × 1.3
want  = 640/9 × Σ (step/15 × fuel) × moderator × chem
chem  = (1 − 0.4 × xenon over 30 %) × (1 − boron/2000)
    BigMachineBlockEntity.vessel · CoreChemistry.powerFactor

    There is no middle temperature

    Heat only builds while the gain beats the cooling; otherwise it stays at 0. So a design is either stable forever or on its way to a meltdown. At 100 % it stops making power, and after 250 ticks (12.5 s) there it explodes, unless it drops under 90 %.

    norm      = min(1, 640 × mod ÷ want)
insertion = min(1, want ÷ (640 × mod))
gain = insertion × 0.7 × heat × (1 + 0.15 MOX)
     + water it can't get × norm × 0.0025
cool = 0.2 + (flow × norm × 0.001 + cooler)
           × (1 − 0.15 × deposits)
heat += (gain − cool) × 0.8 per tick, to 1,000
    DriveCore.tick · BigMachineBlockEntity.controlRods

    Better rods make more steam, not more heat

    Insertion is capped at 1 and the flow cooling is normalised the same way, so tier 4 rods heat the core like tier 1 rods, as long as the pumps and steam generators move all the water they want. Water they can't move is what kills an overdriven core.

    9 × T1 full:  want   640, heat 0.70 vs 0.84
9 × T4 full:  want 8,000, the drive moves 5,120
              heat 1.28 vs 0.61: meltdown
9 × T4 at 9:  want 4,800, heat 0.70 vs 0.84
    DriveCore.tick

    Output follows the charge left

    A shaft burns 1 charge a tick at full depth, times the moderator, and only while its water moves. Its output is worked out from the charge it still has, so a fresh core gives its full FE/t and a core halfway through gives half: over a rod's life, about half the fresh rate. A full rod lasts 48 minutes at full depth, 12 with heavy water.

    burn = step/15 × moderator × chem × moved ÷ wanted
       charges per tick per shaft, top cladding first
rod  = 4 × 14,400 = 57,600 charge
    vessel() vesselBurn · burnRod · FuelRodBlockEntity

    Steam line and turbine

    Two mB of hot primary water make one of wet steam (15 % more with preheated feedwater). A dryer keeps 97.5 % of it, a separator 90 %, a reheater turns 80 % into superheated steam worth half as much again. A turbine takes 400 mB/t; translators raise both its flow and its FE per mB.

    wet   = flow ÷ 2 (× 1.15), ≤ 1,280 per generator
FE/mB = 256 steam, 384 superheated
turbine = 400 mB/t × flow % × FE %
  Brass 150/110 · Composite 200/125 · Glowstone 300/150
    steamGenerator · TurbineControllerBlockEntity

    Hydrogen and xenon, the hidden timers

    A running core makes hydrogen. With no recombiner within 24 blocks it reaches 100 % in about 4 minutes at full power and blows the hull; the second time it takes the reactor room. Xenon left alone stops a full-power core after about 15 minutes, until it falls back under 60 %. The guide barely mentions either.

    hydrogen +0.02 %/t × insertion, −0.012 per recombiner
xenon    +0.012 %/t × insertion (× 1.5 MOX)
         −0.01 % of itself, −0.01 per working filter
filter 600 FE/t, carbon filter lasts 20 min
    CoreChemistry.tick · ignite

    Where the guide and the code disagree

    The Compendium chains a separator into a dryer, but the dryer's input only takes wet steam and the separator puts out dry steam, so that chain may not connect; either one alone makes turbine-ready steam. It also says a drive takes at most 2,560 mB/t; in code that's one steam generator's limit, and the drive's own ports take 5,120.

    dryer accepts(): wet steam only
drive primary ports: 5,120 mB/t
steam generator primary in: 2,560 mB/t
    BigMachineBlockEntity.accepts · cooling_values.json

    When the turbines can't keep up

    Nothing in the chain vents steam. If the turbines take less than the line makes, their tanks fill, then the line's, then the steam generators stop, the hot primary water has nowhere to go and the core is left short of water. This page works out that backed-up state too. It's our reading of the code, and the first thing we'll check in game.

    turbine steam tank 640,000 mB
backed-up flow = turbine intake ÷ steam per mB primary
    steamGenerator · vessel() · TurbineControllerBlockEntity
