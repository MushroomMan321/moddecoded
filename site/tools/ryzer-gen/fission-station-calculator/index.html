      What the code actually says

      Each rod is set by its four neighbours

      Only the channels directly beside a rod count, not diagonals. Graphite adds heat without adding burn, so it is the only way to get more energy out of each rod.

      heat = 1000 × (1 + 0.4·graphite + 0.2·fuel
               − 0.35·control − 0.2·target)
burn =        (1 + 0.2·fuel − 0.35·control
               − 0.2·target)
both at least 0.1; MOX: 2000 heat, 4× the life
      StationReactor.analyse

      Load sets the temperature, temperature sets the turbine

      A rod's heat is split evenly between the coolant channels touching it. Each channel carries 3,000, more with MOX in the core. The harder the coolant works, the hotter the core settles and the better the turbine does.

      capacity = 3000 × (1 + MOX share) [× 1.3 overdrive]
load     = heat ÷ (working channels × capacity)
core °C  = 150 + 450 × load
turbine  = 25% + 15% × load   (20% in overdrive)
      StationReactor.capacity · settledTemperature · efficiency

      "Too hot" on the screen isn't always too hot

      The control screen calls a core too hot if any rod has no coolant beside it, or any one channel is over its capacity, and plans 0 FE/t. The running station is kinder: that heat warms the core, and every working channel then carries it off. So the core only overheats if the total heat is more than all the working channels carry together. This page shows what the station does, and flags when the screen disagrees. It looks unintended, so a later version may close it.

      removed = min(working × capacity × ramp(°C),
              heat − uncooled + stored heat)
stored  = (°C − 20) × 20,000
      StationRunner.tick

      100% isn't the ceiling

      The rating divides by the best layout the mod's own search found, 8,006 FE/t for uranium, and caps at 100%. That layout isn't the ceiling: better ones exist. Finding them is the fun part.

      rating = min(1, planned ÷ (8006 × output%))
MOX reference: 16,012
      StationReactor.BEST_URANIUM · Analysis.rating

      Water: 1 mB for every 50 heat

      Water is cheap, so the station drinks a lot of it: 1 mB per 50 thermal FE carried off, 1 per 25 in overdrive. The reference core needs 420 mB/t, five intake pumps. The station's own tank holds 16 buckets, about 2 seconds of that, so the pumps have to keep up.

      water mB/t = heat ÷ (200 ÷ (4 × water_use%))
overdrive: 8 in place of 4
pumps      = water ÷ pump_rate (100 mB/t)
      StationRunner.heatPerWater · IntakePumpBlockEntity

      One cable side takes 1,000 FE/t

      The energy port pushes everything it holds into the block beside it. A Ryzer Gen energy cable only takes 1,000 FE/t in on a side unless that side has a fitting. The reference core makes 8,006, just over a silver fitting's 8,000. Anything the cable can't take fills the station's 2M FE buffer, and once that's full the rest is lost.

      none 1,000 · silver ×8 · busbar ×32 · cryogenic ×1024
      CableUpgrade · EnergyCableBlockEntity.energyFor

      Overdrive: half as much power again, no SCRAM

      Every rod makes and burns 30% more, every channel carries 30% more, and the turbine tops out at 45%. There's no SCRAM, so an overloaded core heats until it melts down at 1,000°C. Every fuel channel must hold a live rod: after a 10 s grace, a gap starts a 5 minute flux tilt countdown, then the core goes unstable.

      heat, burn, capacity × 1.3
turbine = 25% + 20% × load
tilt    = 10 s grace + flux_tilt_seconds (300)
      StationReactor.OVERDRIVE_HEAT · StationRunner.tick

      SCRAM at 900°C, restart below 400°C

      With the safeties on, a core that can't shed its heat climbs to 900°C, drops its rods and cools to 400°C before it starts again. While it cools, every coolant channel keeps boiling water at full load, and the turbine keeps turning. So an overloaded core loses little: it averages close to what its coolant makes at full load. This page simulates the cycles and shows the average.

      + (heat − removed) ÷ 20,000 °C per tick
SCRAM ≥ 900°C · restart < 400°C
